01

The situation

Brightline is a customer of ours. One office, four floors, 400 desks, about 600 people. Everyone books a desk in the app before they come in, and when they arrive they check in on their phone. Plenty of them don't come in at all.

I get asked whether we can give back a floor when the lease renews, and I genuinely don't know. The app tells me three hundred desks were booked last Tuesday. It doesn't tell me three hundred people turned up, because they didn't.

I export this file every Monday and stare at it. I'd rather have something I can just open and click around in — I get asked about different floors and different months every time.

Dana · Workplace Manager, Brightline

Dana is your user. She isn't technical, she'll open this on a laptop, and whatever she sees has to survive being repeated in a meeting with her finance director.

02

The file

bookings.csv is attached to your email thread. Three months, 17,929 rows, one row per desk booking. Nothing to install, nothing to generate.

ColumnWhat it is
booking_idUnique per booking.
booking_dateThe day the desk was booked for.
desk_id, floorWhich desk, on which of the four floors.
employee_id, teamWho booked it and which team they're on.
booked_atWhen they made the booking.
checked_in_atWhen they actually turned up — blank if they never checked in.

Look at the file before you trust it. Real customer exports always have something in them that makes a naive answer wrong, and this one is no exception. Twenty minutes poking at the data before you write any UI is the right call.

03

Two questions it has to answer

Dana should be able to get both of these out of your screen without asking you anything.

1

How full is each floor, really?

By floor and by day of the week. The screen has to make clear what you counted — Dana repeats that to a finance director and it has to hold up.

2

How much space is booked and never used?

How many desk-days go to no-shows, and whether it's concentrated anywhere — a floor, a day, a few teams. Teams, not named people.

04

Build it as an app

Not a notebook, not a static export — a screen Dana opens and clicks around in. We're not prescribing a layout and we don't care what it looks like, but these seven things have to be true of it.

#Requirement
1One screen. Everything on a single page. No navigation maze, no wizard, no tabs you have to hunt through.
2Controls that actually filter. At minimum a date range and a filter by floor. Every number and chart on the page updates when they change.
3A summary strip. The three or four headline numbers, visible without scrolling, reflecting the current filters.
4At least one chart and one table — and they have to agree with each other. A chart whose total doesn't match the table beneath it is the fastest way to lose a customer's trust.
5A way down to the rows. Click a floor, a day or a team and see the bookings behind that number.
6 Honest states. Something sensible while it loads, something clear when a filter matches nothing, and a visible flag when the data for the selected period can't be trusted.
7Words Dana understands. No column names as labels, no checked_in_at on screen. Your definition of a used desk appears in the interface, not only in your README.

Fine, and encouraged

  • Any framework, or none. Plain HTML and JavaScript is a perfectly good answer.
  • A component or chart library. Generating the whole UI with an AI tool.
  • Pre-computing the numbers into a JSON file instead of parsing the CSV in the browser.
  • Running locally from your README. No deployment needed.

Not needed — skip it

  • Login, accounts, permissions, or a settings page.
  • A backend, a database, or an API. A single file that opens in a browser is fine.
  • A mobile layout. She's on a laptop.
  • Saving state, exporting, dark mode, animation, or visual polish.

Requirement 6 is the one people skip, and it's the one we look at first. An interface that shows a confident number for a period where the underlying data is broken is worse than one that says it doesn't know.

What to send us

One

The app

A repo we can run from your README — one command, or just opening a file. Deploy it if that's easy for you; it isn't required.

Two

A few sentences to Dana

What you found, what you assumed, what you'd add next. Written to her, not to us. A paragraph or two is plenty.

RoughlySpend it on

Three hours is a ceiling, not a target. Requirements 1–5 working cleanly beats all seven half-wired — if you run out of time, say which ones you didn't get to and what you'd do. That's a good answer, not a bad one.

06

You're done when

  • Someone opens it, changes a filter, and both questions get answered without you in the room.
  • The chart and the table agree.
  • Clicking a number gets you to the bookings behind it.
  • An empty filter says so instead of showing zero as if it were a fact.
  • Your definition of a used desk is visible on screen.
  • Your note says what you assumed and where you might be wrong.

  • Don't name individual employees as the problem. Teams, not a wall of shame.
  • Don't hand in something you can't explain.
  • Don't spend eight hours on a three-hour exercise. We can tell, and it doesn't help you.
  • Do email us questions. We reply as Dana, and asking counts in your favour — especially on day one.
